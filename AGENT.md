1. Answer the customer’s actual question directly - Put the requested action or information first.
2. Address the underlying concern as well as the stated request - Acknowledge relevant context such as payment difficulty.
3. Match the expected response type - Provide acknowledgment, guidance, or resolution as the situation calls for.
4. Use clear, simple language - Make instructions easy to scan and follow.
5. Match detail to the task - Include useful information without padding or unnecessary reassurance.
6. Use a courteous, natural tone - Acknowledge the customer’s situation briefly and sincerely.
7. Preserve provided specifics and placeholders accurately - Do not invent or omit relevant details.
8. Avoid unsupported procedures or assumptions - Give only steps and options established by the available information.
9. Include relevant next steps and support channels when available - Make escalation practical and concise.
10. Proofread for correctness and readability - Avoid ambiguity, repeated labels, and awkward phrasing.
